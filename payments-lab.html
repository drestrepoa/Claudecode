Glossary

The terms you will hear in the market.

AP2, Agent Payments Protocol. Open protocol (Google and partners, 2025) for payments initiated by agents. Signed mandates prove what the user allowed; verifiers check them in code. Payment-method agnostic: cards, bank transfers, stablecoins.
UCP, Universal Commerce Protocol. Open standard (Google, Shopify and others, 2026) for agents to discover a merchant's capabilities, run a checkout and receive order events. AP2 mandates plug into its checkout.
Mandate. A signed, tamper-evident record of an authorisation. Closed: a specific checkout and payment. Open: constraints the agent must stay within. AP2 uses SD-JWT verifiable credentials.
Checkout mandate, payment mandate. The two AP2 mandates: the first authorises the merchant to complete this checkout, the second authorises the payment for it. Linked by the hash of the merchant-signed checkout.
Trusted surface. The non-agentic screen where the user sees what they sign: a wallet, a bank app, a passkey prompt. The LLM never sits there.
Credential provider. The holder of the user's payment credentials: a wallet or a bank. It verifies the payment mandate and hands the agent a scoped credential, never the raw card.
Agentic token. Mastercard Agent Pay's credential: a network token bound to a registered agent, a merchant scope, an amount and the user's consent. Europe's first live one ran in 2026 with a processor and an issuing bank.
SEPA Instant. Euro credit transfer in seconds, account to account. The agent initiates through a licensed provider; the user's strong customer authentication is the signature on the mandate.
USDC, x402. A dollar stablecoin and an HTTP-native payment standard: the merchant answers 402 Payment Required, the agent pays with a signed transfer authorisation, a facilitator settles it on-chain. AP2's crypto extension.
Human present, human not present. The user signs the exact checkout; or signs limits once and the agent signs within them. Verifiers always receive a closed mandate; what changes is whose key signed it.
Receipt. Signed answers from the merchant and the payment processor: accepted or rejected, and why. With the mandates, the evidence in a dispute.
FX, settlement currency. The checkout is in the merchant's currency; the user's limit is in theirs. Someone converts, at a rate, for a fee, and bears the risk until settlement: the issuer for cards, the wallet for stablecoins, nobody for SEPA, which is euro only.
